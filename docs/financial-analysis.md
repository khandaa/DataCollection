Here's your investment analysis framework — five tabs to walk through any opportunity systematically:

Value model — Rate five criteria (price-to-intrinsic-value, FCF yield, ROIC, balance sheet, management) weighted by importance. Buffett's margin of safety principle is built in.

Growth model — Score addressable market, revenue consistency, pricing power, reinvestment quality, and secular tailwinds. Munger's inversion principle guides the framing — always ask what could go wrong.

Moat analysis — Click to select which competitive advantages the business actually has (switching costs, network effects, brand, etc.). The framework rates moat width based on how many sources of advantage are stacked together.

Risk checklist — Twelve risks ranked by severity (high/medium/low), from Howard Marks' core insight that risk is permanent loss of capital, not just volatility. Psychological bias is on the list too.

Final verdict — A composite score across all four dimensions, weighted 30/30/25/15, with a recommendation aligned to what Buffett, Munger, or Marks would say at each score level.

A few things to keep in mind as a new investor:

The framework rewards discipline over excitement. A business that scores 9/10 on growth but 3/10 on value is not a buy — it's a speculation. Marks would call that chasing the narrative. The composite score forces you to hold all four dimensions at once.

Start by using it on businesses you already understand well, so you can calibrate your own scoring intuition before applying it to unfamiliar territory.
